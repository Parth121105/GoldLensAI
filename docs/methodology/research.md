# Research engine (Phase 4)

Implementation: [`backend/app/research/`](../../backend/app/research/).
Tests: `backend/tests/test_research_*.py`. All test data is synthetic and
generated by [`tests/synthetic_market.py`](../../backend/tests/synthetic_market.py).

## Research question

> After specification-aware normalization, does the relative price of two MCX
> gold contracts deviate from its recent history in a way that reliably
> reverses, by more than the cost of trading it?

The engine is built so that "no" or "not enough evidence" are normal answers.

## Hypothesis under test

For a pair of contracts A and B, define the spread on each date as

```
spread_bps = 10,000 × ln( A_INR_per_g_999 / B_INR_per_g_999 )
```

**H1 (reversion).** When the spread is a statistical anomaly against its
trailing baseline, it moves back toward that baseline over the next *h*
eligible observations. That move must be larger than for ordinary
observations, and larger than round-trip costs.

H1 assumes neither that spreads are stationary nor that they mean-revert. It
is tested and can fail. Spreads that shift permanently (for example after a
change in location basis or delivery economics) produce "not supported".

## Comparison rules

| Rule | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Fixed contracts | A series compares exactly one A contract (symbol + expiry) with one B contract. It is never rolled or spliced. |
| Expiry matching | Contracts are paired only if their expiries are at most `max_expiry_gap_days` apart (default 10). A gap of 0 is labelled `same_expiry`; otherwise the pair is labelled `cross_expiry` and the gap is reported. GOLDM (5th) vs the others (month-end) is always cross-expiry. |
| Rollover | When a contract expires its series ends. The next expiry pair starts a new series whose baseline begins empty. |
| Missing data | Only dates where both legs have an observation are used. Dates missing one leg are recorded and never filled. |
| Price field | Close by default (MCX's last-half-hour weighted average). Settlement can be chosen and is flagged as non-executable. |

## Eligibility filters (a date is excluded if any applies)

| Code | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `unverified_spec` | Either leg's specification is unverified (unless explicitly allowed) | Normalization would rest on an unverified unit or purity |
| `volume_unknown` | Either leg has no reported volume | Unknown liquidity is not treated as adequate |
| `volume_below_min` | Either leg's volume < `min_volume` (default 1) | A price with no trades is not an observed transaction price |
| `lifecycle_buffer` | Fewer than `lifecycle_buffer_days` (default 7) calendar days to the nearer expiry | Staggered delivery tender period (last 3–5 trading days per MCX circulars) |

Volume is used only as a minimum activity check. It is not a measure of
order-book depth or executable size.

## Baseline and anomaly levels

For each eligible point, the baseline is the mean and sample standard
deviation of the previous `W` eligible spreads in **the same series**,
strictly before the point. `z = (spread − mean) / std`.

| Level | Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Candidate anomaly | \|z\| ≥ k | Unusual against recent history, but possibly just tick rounding |
| 2. Statistical anomaly | Level 1 and \|spread − mean\| > tick resolution | Unusual by more than the smallest expressible price change |
| 3. Cost-adjusted candidate | Level 2 and \|spread − mean\| > known round-trip cost | Could be worth trading if it reverts fully, given **known** costs |
| 4. Historically validated finding | Pair-level verdict from walk-forward validation (below) | Survived out-of-sample testing |
| 5. Potentially actionable | **Never assigned by the software** | Needs independent checks with live executable quotes, depth and current costs |

Other point statuses: `ineligible`, `insufficient_history` (fewer than W prior
points), and `degenerate_baseline` (zero variation).

`k` and `W` are chosen from a small grid (k ∈ {1.5, 2.0, 2.5},
W ∈ {10, 20}). They are conventional starting values, not facts about the
market, and are selected using training data only.

## Costs

Four components, each measured in basis points per leg-side: brokerage,
exchange transaction charges, statutory taxes and levies, and slippage. A
round trip is four leg-sides (two legs, each entered and exited).

All components start **unknown**. Unknown is not zero. With any component
unknown:
- the round-trip cost is not computed;
- no point can reach level 3;
- the best possible verdict is "supported before costs, costs unknown".

Every supplied value records its source and date, or is labelled "user
assumption". Supply costs as JSON:

```json
{
  "brokerage": {"bps_per_leg_side": 1.0, "source": "Broker tariff PDF", "as_of": "2026-10-01"},
  "exchange_transaction_charges": {"bps_per_leg_side": 0.26, "source": "MCX circular ...", "as_of": "2026-10-01"},
  "statutory_taxes_and_levies": {"bps_per_leg_side": 0.6, "source": "...", "as_of": "2026-10-01"},
  "slippage": {"bps_per_leg_side": 1.5, "source": "user assumption"}
}
```

These example numbers are placeholders showing the format. They are not
researched rates.

## Walk-forward validation

Run separately for each symbol pair (for example GOLDTEN/GOLDPETAL), pooling
all of its expiry-matched series.

1. Sort all eligible dates. The last `holdout_fraction` (30%) is the
   **final holdout**; the rest is the development period.
2. Split the development period into `n_folds + 1` consecutive blocks.
   Fold *f* trains on blocks 1..*f* and tests on block *f+1*, an expanding
   window.
3. **Events.** At a point with a valid baseline, the outcome is measured
   *h* eligible observations later in the same series. Outcome (bps) =
   direction × (spread_t − spread_t+h), where direction is the sign of the
   deviation. Positive means the spread moved back toward the baseline.
   - **Signal events** are levels 2–3. They don't overlap: a new signal starts
     only after the previous signal's outcome date.
   - **Control events** are all other points with a valid baseline.
4. **Purging.** An event counts in a window only if both its date and its
   outcome date fall inside that window.
5. **Selection.** In each training window, choose the (k, W) with the highest
   mean signal outcome among those with at least `min_events` signals. Ties
   go to grid order.
6. **Final test.** Parameters are chosen on the whole development period and
   evaluated once on the holdout. The holdout is never used for selection.
   Sensitivity results for every grid point are reported on the holdout for
   information only.

### Verdicts

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| `insufficient_data` | Folds and a holdout can't be formed, or the holdout has fewer than `min_events` (20) signals |
| `not_supported` | Holdout mean ≤ 0, or t < 2, or not above the control mean, or a majority of folds not positive |
| `supported_before_costs_costs_unknown` | Gross evidence supports H1 but at least one cost component is unknown |
| `not_supported_after_costs` | Net mean ≤ 0 or net t < 2 |
| `historically_validated` | All of the above pass with fully known costs. Still a historical result, not a recommendation |

The t-statistic is mean / (sd / √n) over non-overlapping signal events. It is
descriptive. Events in different series on nearby dates may still be
correlated.

## What the tests prove (on synthetic data)

| Scenario | Expected verdict |
|---|---|
| Spikes that revert next day, small known costs | `historically_validated` |
| Same spikes, unknown costs | `supported_before_costs_costs_unknown` |
| Same spikes, costs larger than the spike | `not_supported_after_costs` |
| Level shifts that persist | `not_supported` |
| One month of data | `insufficient_data` |

Leakage tests:
- Rewriting every holdout price leaves the fold results and the chosen
  parameters unchanged.
- Changing future spreads doesn't change past assessments.
- Events that cross a window boundary are excluded.

These results describe the generated data only. They say nothing about MCX
markets.

## Known limitations

- **No real data has been analysed yet.** Every result so far comes from
  synthetic test data.
- The calendar-day lifecycle buffer approximates the trading-day tender
  period, because exchange holidays are not modelled.
- Location basis (GOLDPETAL is Ex-Mumbai), delivery making charges and carry
  between different expiries are not modelled in the spread.
- Bhavcopy close is an end-of-day value, not a synchronized executable quote.
  Two legs' closes may not have been tradable at the same moment.
- One hypothesis (reversion at a fixed horizon) is tested. Other hypotheses
  would need their own validation runs.
